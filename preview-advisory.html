02   Client Questions

Three Questions Your Clients Will Ask You This Week.

Ben Charoenwong · Cofounder · 15 Jun 2026

"Should I be worried about my tech concentration?"

The risk is not that semis are falling; it is that the leadership is narrowing. The rebound was real but only 18 of 39 markets were positive, and US semi-equipment is up +82.4% YTD with daily fatigue showing. A cap-weighted index quietly concentrates you in exactly that thinning leadership.

Evidence to cite: Parallax flagged fatigue divergence in US and Korea semiconductor baskets this week.

"Does gold still belong in the portfolio here?"

Gold miners were the week's other leadership leg, with Australia and Canada precious-metals baskets up roughly +9% WTD as bullion held near records. With the 10-year near a one-year high, a real-asset hedge is doing work that bonds alone are not.

Evidence to cite: Australia Junior Gold Miners +9.4% WTD, Canada Precious Metals Miners +9.0% WTD (Parallax).

"What does today's Fed decision mean for my bonds?"

A hold is almost fully priced; the swing factor is the dot plot and whether Warsh signals the next move is a hike. Until that resolves, duration stays under pressure and rate-sensitive property remains the funding source.

Evidence to cite: 10-year near 4.47–4.55%, Hong Kong property developers −3.5% WTD (Parallax).

04   What Other Advisors Aren't Saying Yet

Three Contrarian Reads Worth Raising With Clients.

“Your index fund just doubled down on the trade that whipsawed.”

The selloff-and-rebound looked like a round trip, but cap-weighting means the rebound rebuilt the same concentration. Most advisors will tell clients "we held through it"; few will say the holding is now more concentrated in a narrowing leadership.

“Gold isn't a doomsday trade this cycle; it's the bond substitute that's working.”

With real yields high and the 10-year near a one-year peak, miners led the week alongside semis. The uncomfortable truth few raise: a 60/40 leaning only on bonds for ballast missed the hedge that actually moved.

“The oil 'relief' is a position-repricing in disguise.”

De-escalation is good news for consumers, but it just took the geopolitical premium out of energy sleeves in a week. Advisors who framed energy as an inflation hedge owe clients the other side of that trade.
