01   This Week

A Hawkish Fed, a Chip Record, and Oil Into the $70s.

Ben Charoenwong · Cofounder · 22 Jun 2026 · window 15–18 Jun 2026

The Fed held, but Warsh turned hawkish.

Reuters · CNBC · Parallax telemetry

The FOMC held at 3.50–3.75% but stripped the easing bias; the dot-plot median rose to 3.8% and futures moved the next move toward an October hike. Duration sleeves stay under pressure into a higher-for-longer path.

Chips closed the week at a SOX record; Intel ran on an Apple deal.

CNBC · Bloomberg · Parallax telemetry

The SOX ran more than 6% to a record and Intel jumped ~9% on an Apple chip deal, but Parallax shows leadership narrowing to Taiwan and US semis. A client on a cap-weighted tech sleeve owns a thinning, not broadening, leadership.

Oil slides toward $79 as the Strait of Hormuz reopens.

Reuters · CNBC · Parallax telemetry

02   Client Questions

Three Questions Your Clients Will Ask You This Week.

Ben Charoenwong · Cofounder · 22 Jun 2026

"Should I be worried about my tech concentration?"

The risk is not that semis are falling; it is that the leadership is narrowing. The chip rally hit a record but only 10 of 39 markets were green on the 18th, and South Korea's crowded cyclicals fell −5.9% WTD. A cap-weighted index quietly concentrates you in exactly that thinning leadership.

Evidence to cite: only 10 of 39 markets positive; KR Cyclical Value Rotation −5.9% WTD on a +77% YTD base (Parallax).

"Does gold still belong in the portfolio here?"

Gold miners ran early in the week, with Australia Junior Gold Miners up +10.7% WTD as bullion held near recent highs, though the leg faded late. With real yields high and the front end repricing, a real-asset hedge still does work that bonds alone are not.

Evidence to cite: Australia Junior Gold Miners +10.7% WTD; gold ~$4,375 Monday, still ~20% below the January record (Parallax / CNBC).

"What does the hawkish Fed mean for my bonds?"

The 2-year just had its biggest Fed-day jump since 2008 as the committee dropped its easing bias and the next move is now priced as a possible hike. Until that resolves, duration stays under pressure and rate-sensitive property remains the funding source.

Evidence to cite: 2-year +16bp Wednesday near 4.18%, 10-year ~4.45%, Hong Kong property developers −7.3% WTD (CNBC / Parallax).

04   What Other Advisors Aren't Saying Yet

Three Contrarian Reads Worth Raising With Clients.

“A record SOX with only 10 of 39 markets green is fragile, not healthy.”

The chip record looks like strength, but breadth was the narrowest in weeks and Korea's crowded cyclicals rolled over −5.9%. Most advisors will point at the record; few will say the index is leaning on a handful of names just as the Fed turns hawkish.

“Gold isn't a doomsday trade this cycle; it's the bond substitute that's working.”

With real yields high and the 10-year near a one-year peak, miners led the week alongside semis. The uncomfortable truth few raise: a 60/40 leaning only on bonds for ballast missed the hedge that actually moved.

“The bond 'safe haven' just took its biggest hit since 2008.”

The 2-year's 16bp Fed-day spike is the kind of move most clients assume only equities make. Advisors who framed the long bond as ballast owe clients an honest read on duration risk if the next Fed move is a hike, not a cut.
