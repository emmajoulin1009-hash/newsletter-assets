01   This Week

Three Moves Reshaping the Client Conversation.

Ben Charoenwong · Cofounder · 15 Jun 2026

US Strikes Iran a Second Day; Oil Jumps, Stocks Sell Off.

Reuters · CNBC · Al Jazeera · EIA

US forces struck Iran for a second day on 10 June and Iran retaliated against US facilities in Bahrain, Jordan and Kuwait. The S&P 500 fell 1.6% and the Nasdaq 2% as risk-off flows ran to defensives. Brent climbed above $96 on a near-total Strait of Hormuz closure, and US crude inventories fell 7.2M bbl, a seventh straight weekly draw.

For client books the signal is the bid for safety, not the oil spike. A constrained Hormuz keeps a floor under inflation into the 16–17 June FOMC, which matters more for the duration sleeve than the energy weight. Treat energy as an inflation hedge to hold, and let real yield and short duration do the rate work. The next review call is the moment to pressure-test duration and single-factor concentration.

Evidence

S&P −1.6% · Nasdaq −2% (10 Jun) · Brent above $96 · Hormuz near-total closure · US crude −7.2M bbl (7th draw) · Defensive +1.5% WTD

CPI Runs Hot at 4.2% Headline, but Core Cools to 2.9%.

BLS · CME FedWatch · CNBC

May CPI rose +0.5% MoM and +4.2% YoY, the hottest headline since April 2023, with energy roughly 60% of the rise. Core was the relief: +0.2% MoM and +2.9% YoY, below the 0.3% estimate. The split keeps higher-for-longer intact ahead of the 16–17 June FOMC.

The headline is mostly oil pass-through, so a hawkish read overstates the trend; the soft core says underlying disinflation is intact. The cleaner question for clients is duration: real-yield and short-duration exposure hedges the rate-repricing risk better than reacting to a single energy-driven print.

Evidence

CPI +0.5% MoM / +4.2% YoY (hottest since Apr 2023) · core +0.2% MoM / +2.9% YoY (below 0.3% est) · energy ~60% of the rise · FOMC 16–17 Jun

Until breadth expands past 30 markets, the Asia-semis bounce is mean-reversion, not trend repair.

02   Client Questions

Three Questions Your Clients Will Ask You This Week.

1. "Should I worry about my clients' AI and mega-cap concentration?"

Yes, but frame it as factor risk, not stock risk. The momentum unwind is a regime shift, not a dip: a static hold of the crowded AI/mega-cap book fell −12% over 1–10 June, and the Iran escalation only reinforced the bid for safety. With breadth thin (only 9 of 39 markets positive), the fix is to size the exposure deliberately into the 16–17 June FOMC, not dump it in one week.

Evidence to cite: static AI/momentum book −12% (1–10 June), only 9 of 39 markets positive WTD, US Managed Care +6.26% WTD leading the defensive bid.

2. "Is the bond sleeve safe with the 10-year back above 4.5%?"

The question is not "bonds or no bonds" but "real versus nominal, short versus long." May CPI ran hot at 4.2% headline, but that was mostly oil pass-through; core cooled to 2.9%, so underlying disinflation is intact. A constrained Hormuz keeps a floor under headline inflation, which pressures long-duration nominal sleeves most. Short-duration and TIPS (VTIP) held far better; shifting duration and adding real yield is the cleanest fiduciary upgrade into the 16–17 June FOMC.

Evidence to cite: CPI +4.2% headline / +2.9% core, energy ~60% of the rise, FOMC 16–17 June, VTIP held in the simple book vs long-duration nominal drawdown.

3. "The US is striking Iran and oil is jumping. Do I add energy?"

Probably not the way the headline suggests. Brent is above $96 on a near-total Hormuz closure, but chasing the spike is a directional oil bet, not a hedge. The constrained Strait keeps a floor under inflation, which matters more for the duration sleeve than the energy weight. Treat existing energy as an inflation hedge to hold, not a position to chase, and let real yield do the rate work.

Evidence to cite: Brent above $96, near-total Strait of Hormuz closure, US crude −7.2M bbl (7th straight draw), Parallax Tactical −0.9% WTD on the oil whipsaw.
