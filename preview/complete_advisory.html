01   This Week

Three Moves Reshaping the Client Conversation.

Ben Charoenwong · Cofounder · 15 Jun 2026

Payrolls Shock Reprices the Curve; Chips Whipsaw Into CPI.

BLS · CME FedWatch · CNBC · Parallax telemetry

May nonfarm payrolls printed +172K against an 80K consensus, reviving rate-hike pricing. CME FedWatch year-end hike odds jumped near 72%, the 10-year pushed above 4.5% and the 30-year above 5%. The S&P 500 fell 2.6% on the week, its first weekly loss since March; the Nasdaq shed 4.18% Friday. Monday 8 June rebounded (S&P +0.30%, Micron +9%, Nvidia +2%) as Jensen Huang called the drop a buy; Tuesday 9 June reversed (S&P −0.26%) on Iran headlines.

The move was in the discount rate, not the companies: duration sleeves built for 2026 cuts took a mark-to-market hit, and higher-for-longer compressed the AI and mega-cap multiples that led all year. CPI lands Wednesday 10 June (near +4.2% YoY, +2.9% core); the FOMC meets 16–17 June, with futures pricing a near-certain hold (~98%) but a ~30% December hike. The next review call is the moment to pressure-test duration and single-factor concentration.

Evidence

NFP +172K vs 80K · Fed hike odds ~72% (CME FedWatch) · 10Y above 4.5%, 30Y above 5% · S&P −2.6% WTD · Nasdaq −4.18% Friday · Defensive +1.5% WTD

Israel-Iran Whipsaws Oil; Hormuz Still Constrained.

Reuters · CNBC · Al Jazeera

Brent crossed roughly $98 then fell back toward $88–94 as Iran said it had ended operations and a US-brokered ceasefire held intermittently. Strait of Hormuz traffic stayed below pre-war levels and OPEC+ added 188K bpd for July. The round-trip, not the spike, is the signal: supply risk is real but the premium is unstable.

That argues against chasing energy on a headline and for holding it as an inflation hedge, not a directional oil bet. A constrained Hormuz keeps a floor under inflation into the CPI print and FOMC, which matters more for the duration sleeve than the energy weight: real-yield and short-duration exposure, not a tactical energy add, is what hedges the rate-repricing risk.

Evidence

Brent ~$98 then $88–94 · Strait of Hormuz below pre-war levels · OPEC+ +188K bpd July · ceasefire fragile, renewed-strike headlines Tuesday

A Defensive-Quality Bid, Not a Broad Rebound.

Parallax telemetry (as of 9 June)

Only 9 of 39 markets closed positive on the week, so the tape repaired far less than the index suggests. US Managed Care led at +6.26% WTD, Hong Kong Aerospace & Defense ran +8.0%, and Asia semis (Taiwan Passive Components +5.0%, Korea Semi Equipment) bounced on short-covering without confirmation. Momentum long/short broke −4.2% in France and −2.2% in Germany as money fled crowded growth.

This is concentration risk in disguise: a book that looks diversified by sector can still be one factor deep if its winners all sat in the momentum cluster payrolls just repriced. Leadership now sits in Managed Care, Defense and Staples, sectors with policy-driven cash flows rather than pure rate sensitivity, so the defensive bid is not just a long-duration proxy. Until breadth expands past 30 markets, the Asia-semis bounce is mean-reversion, not trend repair.

Evidence

Only 9 of 39 markets positive WTD · US Managed Care +6.26% WTD · HK Aerospace & Defense +8.0% · Momentum L/S −4.2% France, −2.2% Germany

02   Client Questions

Three Questions Your Clients Will Ask You This Week.

1. "Should I worry about my clients' AI and mega-cap concentration?"

Yes, but frame it as factor risk, not stock risk. The payrolls shock repriced the rate path (10Y above 4.5%, hike odds ~72%), and higher-for-longer compresses the AI and mega-cap multiples, so the static momentum book fell −7.94% over 1–9 June. The fix is to size the exposure deliberately into the 16–17 June FOMC, not dump it in one week.

Evidence to cite: NFP +172K vs 80K, Fed hike odds ~72% (CME FedWatch), Nasdaq −4.18% Friday, static momentum book −7.94% (1–9 June), only 9 of 39 markets positive WTD.

2. "Is the bond sleeve safe with the 10-year back above 4.5%?"

The question is not "bonds or no bonds" but "real versus nominal, short versus long." Long-duration nominal sleeves took the brunt, while short-duration and TIPS (VTIP) held far better. With the 30-year above 5% and the FOMC on 16–17 June, a sleeve built for 2026 cuts is most exposed to a hawkish surprise. Shifting duration and adding real yield is the cleanest fiduciary upgrade.

Evidence to cite: 10Y above 4.5%, 30Y above 5%, FOMC 16–17 June (~98% hold, ~30% Dec hike), VTIP held in the simple book vs long-duration nominal drawdown.

3. "Does the Israel-Iran oil spike change my energy exposure?"

Probably not the way the headline suggests. Brent crossed ~$98 then round-tripped toward $88–94 on a fragile ceasefire, so chasing the spike is a directional oil bet, not a hedge. A constrained Hormuz keeps a floor under inflation, which matters more for the duration sleeve than the energy weight. Treat existing energy as a hedge to hold, not a position to chase.

Evidence to cite: Brent ~$98 then $88–94, Strait of Hormuz below pre-war levels, OPEC+ +188K bpd July, Parallax Tactical −0.9% WTD on the oil whipsaw.
